  Science Objectives
   * Look for habitability: 
       Identify past environments capable of supporting microbial life
   * Seek biosignatures:
       Seek signs of possible past microbial life in those habitable 
       environments, particularly vertain types of rocks known to preserve 
       signs of life over time
   * Cache samples:
       Collect core rock and "soil" samples and store them on the Martian 
        surface
   * Prepare for humans:
       Test oxygen production from the Martian atmosphere

 INGENUITY (MARS HELICOPTER)
  This experimental autonomous aircraft, carried to Mars attached to the 
  belly of the Perseverance rover,  will test powered flight in the thin
  Martian atmosphere, which is about 1% as thick as Earth's, or comparable
  to an altitude of 100,000 feet on Earth. 
 
  Counter-rotating blades spin at 2400 rpm. Equipped with computers, 
  navigation sensors, internal heaters, and two cameras (color and 
  black-and-white), this engineering test aircraft will provide information 
  to inform decisions on small helicopters for future Mars missions.

   Dimensions: 0.49 meters (~19 inches)
   Rotor span: 1.2 meters (~4 feet)
   Mass      : 1.8 kg
   Power     : solar, self-recharging

  After receiving commands from Earth, relayed by Perseverance rover, the
  test flight(s) are performed without further real-time input.
