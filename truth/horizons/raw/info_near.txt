 BACKGROUND
  The NEAR-Shoemaker spacecraft studied the near-Earth asteroid 433 Eros from 
  orbit over one year. 230 orbits were completed before the spacecraft was set 
  down in the north pole saddle region.

  Mission objectives at Eros were to assess bulk properties: shape, composition,
  mineralogy, internal mass distribution (gravity field), and magnetic field. 
  Other studies included regolith properties, interactions with solar wind, 
  spin state, and any ongoing processes indicated by dust or gas.

  While en-route to Eros, the spacecraft flew past the asteroid 253 Mathilde at
  a distance of 1211 km, imaging 60% of its surface and determining the mass 
  parameter (GM).

  The first attempt at Eros orbit insertion failed, requiring the spacecraft
  orbit the Sun for another year before making a second, successful attempt.

  Upon completion of this first in NASA's Discovery-class series, 
  NEAR-Shoemaker became the first spacecraft to land on an asteroid, 
  where it was left.
