Changes:
    The build makes a new image from the original image. It takes one brightness profile along the long axis of
    the ring ellipses. Each profile value is the mean of 17 image rows. The build resamples the profile to 2048
    pixels between the inner radius and the outer radius. The radius scale comes from a fit to ring edges with a
    known radius (field radiusFit). The alpha value of a pixel is its brightness divided by the largest brightness
    of the strip. The colour of a pixel is the mean colour of 9 image columns around it. The build scales this
    colour to the largest brightness of the strip. A very dark pixel takes the colour of the nearest brighter
    pixel. The build writes 16 equal rows as a PNG file. The small version has 1024 x 8 pixels.

Note of the manifest:
    PNG with transparency (straight alpha, not premultiplied). The horizontal axis is the distance from the centre
    of Saturn: the left edge is at 74,658 km (inner edge of the C ring) and the right edge is at 136,780 km (outer
    edge of the A ring). The radius is linear along the strip (30.33 km per pixel). All rows are equal. The strip
    comes from a Cassini mosaic of 12 December 2004 (PIA06175). The caption says: 'This mosaic of six images
    covers a distance of approximately 62,000 kilometers along the ring plane, from a radius of 74,565 kilometers
    to 136,780 kilometers (46,333 to 84,991 miles) from the planet's center.' and 'This view is from Cassini's
    vantage point beneath the ring plane. The rings are tilted away from Cassini at an angle of about 4 degrees.'
    and 'Images taken using red, green and blue spectral filters were used to create this natural color mosaic.'
    Alpha is the brightness of the image and not a measured opacity (field alphaSource). The colour of each pixel
    times its alpha value gives the brightness of the image again. The colour is a mean over 9 columns of the
    image (field colourSource): in the image the blue channel is less sharp than the red and green channels, and
    this makes colour fringes at sharp ring edges. Inspection of the strip: it shows the C ring (alpha about 0.1
    to 0.5), the B ring (0.6 to 0.95), the Cassini division (0.05 to 0.6) and the A ring (0.5 to 0.65) with the
    Encke gap and the Keeler gap. The colour is warm tan in the B ring, pale pink in the A ring and lavender in
    the C ring and in the Cassini division: the image has these colours. The gaps are not fully clear: their alpha
    is 0.02 to 0.05. The image has a brightness of 5 to 9 of 255 in the gaps and of 1 to 3 in the sky just outside
    of the A ring. My reading: this is stray light. The caption does not say which face of the rings the image
    shows. The dense B ring is the brightest part of the image. So my reading is: the image shows the sunlit face.
    The strip has no D ring and no F ring: the image does not show them. Radius scale: the build fits a straight
    line to five sharp ring edges of the fact sheet (field radiusFit). These five edges agree with the fit to 5
    km. Three other features differ from the fact sheet by 45 km to 88 km: the inner edge of the Maxwell ringlet,
    the inner edge of the B ring (a slow rise in the image) and the inner edge of the A ring (a small step in the
    image). So a ring feature can lie up to about 90 km (3 pixels) from its true place. The fit gives 10.87 km per
    pixel of the original image. The caption says: 'The image scale is 10.5 kilometers (6.5 miles) per pixel.' By
    the fit the image starts at 74,699 km. So the first pixel of the large strip has no image data: its alpha
    value is 0.

Description
Saturn's most prominent feature, its dazzling ring system, takes center stage in this stunning natural color mosaic which reveals the color and diversity present in this wonder of the solar system. Gaps, gravitational resonances and wave patterns are all present, and the delicate color variations across the system are clearly visible.
This mosaic of six images covers a distance of approximately 62,000 kilometers along the ring plane, from a radius of 74,565 kilometers to 136,780 kilometers (46,333 to 84,991 miles) from the planet's center.
This view is from Cassini's vantage point beneath the ring plane. The rings are tilted away from Cassini at an angle of about 4 degrees.
Images taken using red, green and blue spectral filters were used to create this natural color mosaic. The images were acquired using the Cassini spacecraft narrow angle camera on Dec. 12, 2004, at a distance of approximately 1.8 million kilometers (1.1 million miles). The image scale is 10.5 kilometers (6.5 miles) per pixel.
The Cassini-Huygens mission is a cooperative project of NASA, the European Space Agency and the Italian Space Agency. The Jet Propulsion Laboratory, a division of the California Institute of Technology in Pasadena, manages the mission for NASA's Science Mission Directorate, Washington, D.C. The Cassini orbiter and its two onboard cameras were designed, developed and assembled at JPL. The imaging team is based at the Space Science Institute, Boulder, Colo.
For more information about the Cassini-Huygens mission, visit [http://saturn.jpl.nasa.gov] http://saturn.jpl.nasa.gov and the Cassini imaging team home page, [http://ciclops.org] http://ciclops.org.
