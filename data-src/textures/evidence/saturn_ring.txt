    of Saturn. The inner edge is at the left and the outer edge is at the right. The source does not state the
    inner and outer radius. Estimate (not from the source): a fit of the outer edge of the B ring and the outer
    edge of the A ring to the NASA Saturnian Rings Fact Sheet
    (https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html) gives a linear scale of about 35.0 km per
    pixel of the 2048 pixel strip. The left edge of the image is then at about 69,300 km and the right edge is at
    about 141,050 km from the centre of Saturn (1.150 and 2.340 equatorial radii of 60,268 km). With this scale
    the inner edge of the C ring, the inner edge of the B ring, the Encke gap and the Keeler gap agree with the
    fact sheet to about 120 km. The F ring lies about 540 km outside of its fact sheet radius. Landmarks as
    fractions of the width: inner edge of the C ring 0.073, inner edge of the B ring 0.316, outer edge of the B
    ring 0.672, Encke gap 0.896, Keeler gap 0.936, outer edge of the A ring 0.941, F ring 0.990. The small file
    has 1024 x 63 pixels.

Textures in this pack are based on NASA elevation and imagery data. Colors and shades of the textures are tuned accordng to true-color photos made by Messenger, Viking and Cassini spacecrafts, and, of course, the Hubble Space Telescope.

NOTES:

Some parts of the planets remain to be mapped. These “gaps” are filled with fictional terrain that corresponds with the rest of the landscape.

The colors are slightly more saturated in order to highlite a unique nature for each object.

Earth textures are the most precise part of the pack: They are a result of merging and adjusting large amount of geo-data, space photos and images from NASA’s Blue Marble (which might well be the 2nd best collection of Earth’s textures available, so check it out)

True Earth colors (as seen from space) may vary depending on the camera used, duration of exposure, lightning conditions and atmospheric conditions. Textures in our pack are sharp, bright and colorful.

Seasonal variations affect Earth’s flora and snow coverage. Earth in our textures has a lot of vegetation and less snow.

Measurement on public/textures/saturn_ring.png (2048 x 125 pixels, RGBA).
The rows are almost equal (largest difference to the middle row: 11 of 255).
Column positions (0 to 2047) of features in the alpha channel of the middle row:
  inner edge of the C ring      about  150   (alpha rises from 13 to about 45)
  inner edge of the B ring      about  647   (alpha rises from about 170 to about 210)
  outer edge of the B ring      about 1376.0 (alpha falls from 182 to 37)
  Encke gap (inner edge)        about 1830.6 (alpha falls from 179 to 29)
  Keeler gap                    about 1917   (alpha dips from 215 to 180)
  outer edge of the A ring      about 1926.1 (alpha falls from 208 to 31)
  F ring (peak)                 about 2028.6 (alpha peak 80)
Fit of two edges to the fact sheet (B outer edge 117,507 km; A outer edge 136,780 km):
  scale = (136780 - 117507) / (1926.1 - 1376.0) = 35.04 km per pixel
  left edge of the image  (x = 0)    = 117507 - 1376.0 * 35.04 = about 69,300 km
  right edge of the image (x = 2048) = about 141,050 km
Check of the other features with this scale (fact sheet value in brackets):
  C inner edge 74,553 km (74,658); B inner edge 91,966 km (91,975); Encke gap 133,434 km (133,410);
  Keeler gap 136,461 km (136,487); F ring 140,371 km (139,826).
The radius range is an estimate of this work. The source page does not state it.
