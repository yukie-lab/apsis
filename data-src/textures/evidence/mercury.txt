Note of the manifest:
    Enhanced colour (false colour), not natural colour. The red channel shows light of 1000 nm (infrared). The
    green channel shows 750 nm. The blue channel shows 430 nm. The source says: 'This is not what Mercury would
    look like to the human eye, but rather by using a wider range of wavelengths, color differences on Mercury's
    surface can be accentuated beyond those visible to a person.' MESSENGER MDIS mosaic (data set MD3, version 1,
    end of mission). The source says that each pixel is the average of all images that meet its criteria, and that
    the reflectance is corrected to one lighting geometry. So the map has no day side, no night side and few
    shadows. Inspection of the small image: the map shows tan plains, blue-grey plains and bright ray craters. A
    thin strip at the top edge has no data and is black. It is wider between 59 and 88 percent of the width. A
    thin line at the bottom edge is black too. Together they are 0.3 percent of the surface. Near both poles the
    map shows strong shading and stretched pixels. No seam is visible at the left and right edge. The original
    file has 23040 x 11520 pixels.

Abstract
This mosaic shows Mercury's colors as viewed by placing images from MESSENGER's 1000 nm, 750 nm, and 430 nm narrow-band filters in the red, green, and blue channel respectively. This is not what Mercury would look like to the human eye, but rather by using a wider range of wavelengths, color differences on Mercury's surface can be accentuated beyond those visible to a person. The projection defined is Simple Cylindrical centered on 0Â° latitude and 0Â° longitude.
The 3-color Map Projected Multispectral RDR (MD3) data set consists of a mosaicked global color map of 3-color image sets, as reflectance corrected to i = 30Âº, e = 0Âº, and g = 30Âº sampled at a scale of 128 pixels per degree, compiled from images taken as a part of the regional 3-color map campaign. This is version 1 of the map, delivered at end of mission. Instead of the value from any single image being used at a particular pixel location in a given wavelength band, the value used is the average from all of the images at that location where criteria for image scale, photometric geometry, and detector temperature are met.
This mosaic contains data acquired by the MErcury Surface, Space ENvironment, GEochemistry and Ranging (MESSENGER) Mercury Dual Imaging System (MDIS) instrument.
