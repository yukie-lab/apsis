Changes:
    The build fills each row that has no clean image data. The fill south of 22.2 degrees south is the mean colour
    of the nearest valid rows. Near the limit the fill blends from the nearest valid row to this mean colour. Then
    the build enlarges the image from 721 x 361 to 2048 x 1024 pixels. It converts the image from TIFF to JPEG
    (quality 85). It tags the image with the sRGB colour profile.

Note of the manifest:
    Hubble Space Telescope map of 13 September 2015 (OPAL programme). The source page calls this file 'RotB Color
    Preview'. The readme gives for the second rotation: 'Rotation 2 (Sept 13 00:09 to Sept 13 13:37 UT)'. Clouds
    change with time. The readme says: 'Color maps (TIFF only), are generated from the F657N (red), F547M (green),
    and F467M (blue) filters, with slight contrast enhancement.' So the colour is near natural, not exact. The
    latitude is planetographic (readme: 'between -90 and +90 deg planetographic latitude', with 'equatorial and
    polar radii of 25559 and 24973 km'). With these radii the planetographic latitude is larger than the
    planetocentric latitude by up to 1.3 degrees. The source removes the limb darkening of each image (readme:
    'Minnaert correction'). So the map has no day side and no night side. The original file has image data for the
    northern part of the planet only. Its south is black. The edge of the image data has striped colour artefacts.
    The build treats these rows as no data too. So the image data cover the north pole to 22.2 degrees south. The
    build fills the rest (31.1 percent of the surface of a sphere): see the fields latitudeLimitsDeg and
    filledBands. The fill is not an observation. Inspection of the small image: the map is cream-white north of
    about 45 degrees north and pale cyan south of it. It shows almost no cloud features. Four faint brighter
    patches lie at the fill limit (they differ from their row by 10 of 255 at most). No seam is visible at the
    left and right edge. The original file has 721 x 361 pixels (2 pixels per degree; the readme says: 'this
    oversamples the data'): the published image is not sharper than the original. The first column and the last
    column of the original show the same meridian.

OPAL Uranus Cycle 23 Maps READ ME file

Uranus data were acquired with WFC3/UVIS between Sept. 12, 2015 05:04 UT and Sept. 13, 2015 13:37 UT

Images were navigated using an ellipsoid limb fitting technique with equatorial and polar radii of 25559 and 24973 km, respectively.  The FQ727N data were fringe corrected post-pipeline, resulting in an ~1.1% correction. Each map was generated at the sub-earth longitude +/-90 at 2 pixels/deg resolution, between -90 and +90 deg planetographic latitude; this oversamples the data.  Maps were mosaicked to cover 360 deg of longitude (left edge = 0 deg E. longitude, increasing to the right) and seams between maps were interpolated to smooth, where needed.  The table below lists the Minnaert correction (ratio of cosines of incidence and emission angles to the k power) applied to remove limb darkening for each map.  The mosaics are available in TIF (arbitrarily scaled) and FITS.  The FITS values can be converted to I/F by multiplying by the values in the table below.  Two global maps exist for each filter, covering two full rotations of Uranus. Color maps (TIFF only), are generated from the F657N (red), F547M (green), and F467M (blue) filters, with slight contrast enhancement.  

Rotation 1 (Sept. 12 05:04 to Sept. 12 20:07 UT)
Rotation 2 (Sept 13 00:09 to Sept 13  13:37 UT)

Data Products

The data files have the following naming convention:

hlsp_opal_hst_wfc3-uvis_<planet>-<rotation>_<filter>_v1_<type>.<ext>

where:

<planet> = one of "jupiter", "saturn", "uranus", or "neptune"

<rotation> = the rotation+epoch of the observation, in the format of <year><letter>, e.g., "2015a" is the first rotation of Jupiter from Cycle 22, and "2015b" is the second rotation from Cycle 22. Keep in mind that HST cycles span two calendar years since they start in Oct. and run through Sep. of the following year.

<type> = Data product type, either "globalmap" for the science data product and full-size preview images, or "globalmap-medium" and "globalmap-small" for the smaller-sized preview images. Some datasets, like Uranus Cycle 33, introduce a "n-pole" product type, where 0 degrees longitude is oriented to the right. These also include the "n-pole-medium" and "n-pole-small" preview images.

<ext> = File extension, either "fits" for the science data products, "tif" for the preview images, or "png" for the web-friendly version of the preview images.

Data file types:

_globalmap.fits
Mosaic (projection) for the given filter.

_n-pole.fits
Mosaic (projection) for the given filter, with 0 degrees longitude oriented to the right.

_globalmap.tif,

_n-pole.tif

Preview image (size varies by target).

_globalmap-medium.tif,

_n-pole-medium.tif

Preview image (width x height ~ 900 x 450 px, varies by target).

_globalmap-small.tif,

_n-pole-small.tif

Preview image (width x height ~ 450 x 225 px, varies by target).

_globalmap-small.png,

_n-pole-small.png

Web-friendly version of the small-sized preview image.

Most data hosted at MAST are in the public domain (see: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_data] open data), and therefore do not have restrictions on use. There are, however, expectations (or in some cases, requirements) that researchers acknowledge the originating mission and (usually) STScI in all scholarly publications. See [https://archive.stsci.edu/publishing/data-attributions] MAST Data Attributions for details.

The subset of data for which restrictions do apply are described in the following subsections.

Exclusive Access Data

Access to science data from most active missions is often limited to the Program Investigator Team during a period of exclusive access immediately following the observations. The duration of the exclusive access period ranges from a few months to as much as a year, depending upon the mission, the program category, and other factors. Some other data, such as those obtained during facility commissioning, or those that are found to duplicate concurrent observations by a Guaranteed Time Observer (GTO), may also be embargoed for a period of time. Data falling under exclusive access can be discovered via MAST public interfaces, but may not be retrieved except by authorized and authenticated persons. Following the expiration of the applicable exclusive access period, science data become available for public use without restriction.

Principal Investigators may request modifications (i.e., extensions, reductions, and/or restrictions) to the exclusive access period for some or all observations in their observing programs. Please make these requests via the Archive help desk: [mailto:archive@stsci.edu] archive@stsci.edu. The Help Desk staff will forward all requests to the authorizing officials. All requests must be approved by STScI before being enacted.

Licensed Data

Some data products, such as high-level science products ( [http://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/] HLSPs) which are created by the community and are made available through MAST, are covered under a Creative Commons Attribution license ( [https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/] CC BY 4.0). These products are available to the community for their use without restriction, provided that the product providers (i.e., the creators and MAST) are acknowledged in published works. If a user obtains such data from MAST and subsequently provides the data to a third party, the terms of the CC BY 4.0 license convey with the data.
